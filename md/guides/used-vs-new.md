# When used office gear is a gift

Start with gear you already have. An old laptop, or a retired OptiPlex from Marketplace, is the first lab. A cheap used switch can wait until you have something running. It is a trap if it was built for a cold data-center aisle.

If you have not read it yet, start at [start with what you already have](<start-with-what-you-have>). You don't need to spend money to begin. This note is for the next used box, after that old PC is already on.

## Buy used

  * An old laptop or a Marketplace OptiPlex / EliteDesk / office mini PC. That is the first computer. See [start with what you already have](<start-with-what-you-have>).
  * Fanless or near-fanless switches you can identify by exact SKU. A used UniFi Lite or a known 8-port 2.5G is fine if the seller shows the board, not a blurry rack selfie.
  * Small Synology 2-bays that still get DSM updates. Check the model against Synology's current update list before you click.
  * DAC cables, optics, and patch. Commodity. Buy used all day.



## Skip used

  * 1U/2U Dell / HP / Lenovo servers from 2016–2019 as a first lab. Idle is often 80–150W. At $0.15/kWh that is $100–200 a year to host what an N100 does on 12W.
  * Big Cisco Catalyst with default fans. The ports are lovely. The noise is not. You will move it to the garage and then you will not use it.
  * Mystery “10G card” listings with no chipset. You want an Intel X710 / XXV710 or a known Mellanox SKU, not “works great.”



## Before you click buy

If you can't name the idle watts, the NIC chipset, and where the next firmware lives, it isn't a bargain. It's a project. Projects are fine. They just aren't the default buy.
