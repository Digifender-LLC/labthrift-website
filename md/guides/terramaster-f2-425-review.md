# Cheap quiet 2-bay when you want Intel and don't need Synology DSM: TerraMaster F2-425

Cheap quiet 2-bay file box with one faster-than-house ethernet port when you want Intel without Synology DSM. Official chip is N5095. No M.2. Runs TerraMaster TOS, not DSM.

Cheap quiet 2-bay file box when you want Intel and don't need Synology DSM (DSM is Synology’s software). Entry Intel against ARM toys and against a DS224+. Official store specification: Intel Celeron N5095, 4C, 2.0 GHz burst 2.9 GHz, 4 GB DDR4 non-ECC (one slot, official max 16 GB), one 2.5GbE, two SATA bays (3.5" / 2.5" HDD or 2.5" SSD), no M.2, HDMI, USB 3.2 (2× 10 Gbps + 1× 5 Gbps), TRAID plus RAID 0 / 1 / JBOD / Single. 48 W brick. 2-year warranty. 222 × 119 × 154 mm. Tool-free Push-Lock trays.

CPU labeling is a mess on retailer pages. Some Newegg tables and even the TerraMaster store HTML title have said “N95.” The official specification block on the product page says **N5095**. Quote that. Don't copy Newegg’s F4-425 “N95” row onto this chassis.

Noise claim: 19.0 dB(A) standby, 2 SATA drives, 1 m, 17.3 dB(A) room. Power: official comparison table on the same store lists 22 W access / 11 W hibernation; Neowin measured 22 W / 11 W with 2× IronWolf 4 TB `ST4000VN008`. Those 4 TB IronWolfs are the 5400-class capacity trap. IronWolf 1–6 TB is 5400 RPM; don't assume the badge means 7200. See [CMR picks](<nas-hdd-picks-2026-cmr>).

Aging N5095, no NVMe. That is the honesty. You are buying a 2.5G port and an x86 board that can run TOS 6 (store copy still says TOS 6; TOS 7 is being marketed on the same page) for less than a Plus 2-bay. You are not buying DSM, M.2 cache, or a 2024 Intel N-series. HDMI and TRAID are the extras. TOS vs DSM: if the restore has to work on a Sunday, read [the existing Synology slot](<first-nas>) first.

## Skip

Skip if you need NVMe, dual LAN, or DSM. Skip if the storefront is out of stock and the only Amazon hit is a drive bundle. Skip SMR desktop disks in TRAID: WD Red `EFAX`, Blue `EZAZ`, BarraCuda 3.5" 4 / 8 TB. Skip treating two 5400 RPM disks as a VM datastore.
