Two floors, one gigabit house, cheap af power: EAP613

AX1800 ceiling AP (a Wi-Fi radio) on cheap 802.3af power. No DC brick in the box.

Two floors, one gigabit house, cheap af power. An AP is a ceiling or wall Wi-Fi radio. The current Omada floor SKU is still a 2×2 Wi-Fi 6 ceiling puck with a gigabit port and a 10.9 W PoE draw. A cheap 802.3af injector (the small PoE power class) or a 4-port 64 W fanless switch is enough. No DC brick in the box.

The trap is Amazon copy that says “PoE+ powered.” V2 is 802.3af, 48 V passive, or 12 V DC. The adapter is not in the box. 48 V passive is not 24 V. A leftover airMAX injector is the wrong brick. See the 24 V trap note.

Add the AP draw before you celebrate a 52 W UniFi Lite 8. Two EAP613s are ~22 W. Two EAP613s plus two cheap cameras will still surprise a thin budget. For the injector-vs-switch math, use one injector, two injectors, or a 4-port switch.

Skip

Skip the 5-pack listing (B0C29X1397) unless you actually need five. Skip EAP653 UR. Listing copy mixed Wi-Fi 7 marketing onto an AX3000 SKU. Skip a $190 Wi-Fi 7 puck until the WAN is faster than the AP; that is the Wi-Fi 7 tax.
