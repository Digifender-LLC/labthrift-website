Living-room dual-port mini vs closet four-port appliance

A living-room box with one or two network ports versus a closet four-port appliance. A USB faster-port dongle is usually a worse idea than buying the appliance once.

A living-room dual-port mini versus a closet four-port appliance. 2.5G means faster house ethernet. The roundup puts both classes in one table. This is the decision tree. Living-room dual-NIC: EQ14 (1G or 2.5G variants, $375 official / Amazon ~$189 snapshot), EQ12 (2× 2.5G, Newegg live), AOOSTAR N1 Pro (2× 2.5G, soldered 12 GB, $137–$175). 4-NIC: VP2430e $419; CWWK B0CL5SBWCJ barebone. Single-NIC control: G3 Plus / S12 Pro (1× 2.5G or 1× 1G).

Buy dual-LAN if the box is also the TV node and you only need WAN + LAN. Buy 4-NIC if you will isolate IoT / lab / WAN without a USB dongle. USB 2.5G NICs drop, reset, and fight suspend. They are a weekend, not a closet plan.

Skip

Skip a USB 2.5G dongle as the “fourth port.” Skip AliExpress-only Topton. Skip sold-out Minisforum MS-01 / UN100L as hero buys. Skip 8 GB soldered traps. If you need 10G SFP+ on N-class silicon that still ships, VP2440e. N150 will not saturate two 10G links with IDS.
